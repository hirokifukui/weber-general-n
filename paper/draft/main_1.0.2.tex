\begin{abstract}
Let $B_n=\Q(2\cos(2\pi/2^{n+2}))$ be the $n$-th layer of the cyclotomic $\Z_2$-extension of $\Q$, $h_n$ its class number and $k_n=h_n/h_{n-1}$. Combining the saturation description of $k_n$ by Kashio and Yoshizaki with the height floor of Morisawa and Okazaki and Blichfeldt's theorem, applied to the lattice of a single saturation component, we obtain for every layer $n\ge2$ and every odd prime $\ell$ the criterion $\ell^{\,\ord_{2^n}(\ell)}>C_n\Rightarrow\ell\nmid k_n$, with $C_n=2(4/\pi)^{2^{n-2}}\Gamma(2+2^{n-2})\prod_{\chi}|L(1,\chi)|/\log(2+\sqrt5)^{2^{n-1}}$, the product over the even characters of conductor $2^{n+2}$. At $n=7$: $\ell\nmid h_7/h_6$ for $\ell\equiv63,65,127\pmod{128}$, $\ell>1.32\times10^{15}$, and for $\ell\equiv1\pmod{128}$, $\ell>1.73\times10^{30}$. For the $1000$ primes $\ell\equiv65\pmod{128}$ with $10^9<\ell\le1001287361$, which Kashio and Yoshizaki excluded conditionally on their Conjecture~2.2, the exclusion holds unconditionally, through finite certificates checked by a read-only verifier. The criterion is an instance of an abstract saturation--height bound (Theorem~SH), which also gives, from an explicit bound for $|L(1,\chi)|$, a computation-free criterion at every layer, and, in the cyclotomic $\Z_3$-tower, a layer-fixed bound resting on the relative-norm-one Horie unit and on the exact covolume of its logarithmic lattice, computed by a character product and positive at every layer; at the layer of degree $81$ this bound lies below the uniform bound of Morisawa and Okazaki in both classes $\ell\equiv\pm1\pmod{81}$. Designated finite computations and discrete cores are kernel- or certificate-checked; every external literature input is isolated as an explicit hypothesis.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\subsection{Weber's problem and its known controls}
Let $B_n=\Q(X_n)$, $X_n=2\cos(2\pi/2^{n+2})$, be the layer of degree $2^n$ in the cyclotomic $\Z_2$-extension of $\Q$, $h_n$ its class number and $k_n=h_n/h_{n-1}$. Weber showed that $h_n$ is odd; Weber's class number problem asks whether $h_n=1$ for all $n$. It is known that $h_n=1$ for $n\le6$ (van der Linden for $n\le5$; Miller \cite{Mi} Thm.~2.1 for $n=6$), and Miller \cite{Mi} Thm.~2.2 gives $h_7=1$ under the generalized Riemann hypothesis, using in its last step the unconditional exclusion of all $\ell<10^9$ by Fukuda and Komatsu \cite{FK3}.

Three kinds of control on the odd primes dividing $h_n$ are in the literature, and they fix different variables.
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] \emph{Fix $\ell$, climb $n$.} Washington \cite{Wa75,Wa78}: the exact power of $\ell$ dividing $h_n$ is constant for large $n$ (the case $p=2$ is \cite{Wa75}); the argument is effective in the sense that a computable stabilization bound can be extracted from it (\cite{Wa78}); no numerical specialization of that bound is used here.
\item[(ii)] \emph{Fix a congruence class of $\ell$, all $n$ above a threshold.} Horie \cite{Ho05,Ho07} ($\ell\not\equiv\pm1\pmod 8$), Horie--Horie \cite{HH09} (explicit thresholds $\Theta$ per decomposition field, with the finite-layer clause ``$\ell\nmid h_{n_0}$''), Fukuda--Komatsu \cite{FK3} ($\ell<10^9$ or $\ell\not\equiv\pm1\pmod{32}$), Morisawa--Okazaki \cite{MO16} ($\ell>G(2,s,f)$; $\ell\not\equiv\pm1\pmod{64}$).
\item[(iii)] \emph{Fix the layer.} Kashio--Yoshizaki \cite{KY} describe $k_n$ through a saturation criterion on the irreducible factors of $x^{2^{n-1}}+1$ over $\F_\ell$, prove an unconditional trace floor $33\cdot2^n$ on the relative units for $n\ge3$, and exclude many primes at $n=7$ conditionally on their Conjecture~2.2.
\end{itemize}
The present paper works at a fixed layer with all odd $\ell$ at once. Its criterion (Theorem~\ref{thm:A}) is componentwise: it applies Blichfeldt's theorem \cite{Bl14} to the lattice of one saturation component and compares against the height floor of \cite{MO16}. Where the criterion does not apply, the same components are searched for finite witnesses; the result is a certificate family whose soundness is a theorem relative to the same literature inputs (Section~\ref{sec:cert}).

The criterion is one instance of an abstract saturation--height bound (Theorem~\ref{thm:SH}) with three inputs: a \emph{carrier} (a saturation statement producing an $\ell$-th root of a Galois-module element), a \emph{floor} (a lower bound on the height of such roots) and a \emph{lattice} (Blichfeldt's theorem on the logarithmic image). The $\Z_2$-tower supplies the carrier through Kashio--Yoshizaki, the floor through \cite{MO16} and the lattice through Dirichlet's unit theorem; the cyclotomic $\Z_3$-tower supplies the same three inputs through Horie's saturation lemma, the relative-norm-one height floor of \cite{MO16} (the Horie unit has relative norm $1$) and the exact covolume of the Horie-unit lattice, which is computed by a character product and is positive at every layer (Section~\ref{sec:p3}). In the $\Z_3$-tower this line of study goes back to Morisawa \cite{Mo12}, who bounded the problem through the Mahler measure of the Horie unit. One theorem, two towers; the layer-fixed constants obtained in the second tower lie below the uniform-in-$n$ bound of \cite{MO16} in both congruence classes at the layer of degree $81$.

\subsection{The four surviving classes at $n=7$}\label{sec:classes}
By \cite{FK3} and \cite{MO16} Cor.~B, every odd prime $\ell$ with $\ell<10^9$ or $\ell\not\equiv\pm1\pmod{64}$ satisfies $\ell\nmid h_n$ for all $n$. Above $10^9$ the classes $\ell\equiv1,63,65,127\pmod{128}$ remain; these are exactly the classes with $\ord_{128}(\ell)\le2$. In the notation of \cite{MO16}, $f=\ord_4(\ell)$ and $2^s\,\|\,\ell^f-1$; Theorem~A of \cite{MO16} excludes $\ell\in D(2,s,f)$ for all $n$ whenever $\ell>G(2,s,f)$, with $G(2,s,f)=\big[2(\sqrt\pi/(\sqrt2\log(2+\sqrt5)))^{c}((c+2)/2)!\big]^{1/f}$, $c=2^{s-1}$. Its proof (\cite{MO16} \S3.3) first obtains, at a fixed layer $n$, the same bound with $c$ replaced by $d=2^{\min(n,s)-1}$, and then substitutes $d\to c$ to make the statement uniform in $n$. \cite{MO16} prints only the values $G(2,6,1)<7.8\times10^{12}$ and $G(2,6,2)<2.8\times10^6$; every other value of $G$ below was evaluated by us from the displayed formula (our evaluation reproduces the two printed bounds: $7.716\times10^{12}$ and $2.778\times10^6$).

Three different mechanisms are visible in Table~\ref{tab:classes}. In class $65$ ($s<n$) the published theorem of \cite{MO16} is sharper than Cor.~\ref{cor:7} by a factor $170$. In class $63$ ($s=n$) the $d\to c$ substitution costs nothing, and Cor.~\ref{cor:7} is sharper than the published $G(2,7,2)$ by the constant alone (a factor $34$, coming from the exact covolume and the component lattice). For general $n$ the same three regimes occur, with $s$ compared to $n$; the position of the range covered by Theorem~A and not by \cite{MO16} is determined by $C_n$, whose computed values for $2\le n\le9$ increase with $n$ (Table~\ref{tab:Cn}); nothing is claimed about $C_n$ for $n\ge10$.

The window $10^9<\ell\le7.716\times10^{12}$ in class $65$ is reached by none of the uniform theorems compared in Table~\ref{tab:classes}; \cite{KY} \S4.2 covers its first $1000$ primes conditionally on Conjecture~2.2. Theorem~\ref{thm:family} covers the same $1000$ primes unconditionally. In the tested $1000$-prime interval, ordinary certificate generation required $8$--$20$ seconds per prime (one prime needed an escalation); no complexity or success-rate claim is made outside this interval. The window itself contains an estimated $4\times10^9$ primes (prime number theorem, equidistribution in the $64$ classes mod $128$); nothing here amounts to an exhaustive treatment of the window.

\subsection{The exponent ring, the log map and the components}
\begin{definition}\label{def:H}
$R_n=\Z[x]/(x^m+1)$, with $x$ acting on $A_n$ through $\sigma$: for $a=\sum_{i<m}a_ix^i$ put $u_a=\prod_{i<m}\sigma^i(\varepsilon_n)^{a_i}\in A_n$. Let $\lambda_j=\log|\sigma^j\varepsilon_n|$ ($j\bmod 2m$); $\lambda_{j+m}=-\lambda_j$ because $\Nr\varepsilon_n=1$. The log map is $H_n:\Z^m\to\R^{2m}$, $(H_na)_j=\sum_{i<m}a_i\lambda_{i+j}$, the log vector of $u_a$ over the $2m=2^n$ real embeddings. For an odd prime $\ell$ and an irreducible factor $f$ of $x^m+1$ over $\F_\ell$ put $w=(x^m+1)/f$, $M_f=w\cdot\F_\ell[x]/(x^m+1)$, and $L_f=\pi^{-1}(M_f)\subseteq R_n$ where $\pi:R_n\to R_n/\ell R_n=\F_\ell[x]/(x^m+1)$. The lattice of the component $f$ is $\Lambda_f=\ell^{-1}H_n(L_f)$.
\end{definition}
By \cite{KY} \S4, $A_n/\{\pm1\}$ is free abelian on the classes of $\sigma^i(\varepsilon_n)$, $0\le i<m$, so $a\mapsto u_a$ identifies $R_n$ with $A_n/\{\pm1\}$ as $\Z[G_n]$-modules. \cite{KY} define $A_n^{1/\ell}=\{x\in\R: x^\ell\in A_n\}$ (real $\ell$-th roots) and identify
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ident}
R_n/\ell R_n=\F_\ell[x]/(x^m+1)\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }A_n^{1/\ell}/A_n,\quad \bar a\mapsto r_a A_n,\quad r_a:=\textstyle\prod_{i<m}\sigma^i(\varepsilon_n)^{a_i/\ell}
\end{equation}
where $r_a$ is the real $\ell$-th root of $u_a$; this is well defined on classes mod $\ell$ because changing $a$ by an element of $\ell R_n$ multiplies $r_a$ by an element of $A_n$. The map $M_f\to A_n^{1/\ell}/A_n$ is the restriction of \eqref{eq:ident} to the subspace $M_f$.

\section{Evaluation of the constant}\label{sec:const}
\subsection{Proof guide for Proposition D}
The characters of $G_n$ with $\chi(\tau)=-1$ are the even characters of $(\Z/q)^\times$ of conductor exactly $q$, determined by $\chi(3)$, which runs over all primitive $2^n$-th roots of unity (Appendix~\ref{app:C}). Part (i) is the group determinant: the Gram matrix $H_n^{\mathsf T}H_n$ is negacirculant, its eigenvectors are $(\omega^i)_{i<m}$ for $\omega^m=-1$, with eigenvalues $|S(\chi)|^2/2$ where $\omega=\chi(3)$. Part (ii) writes $\varepsilon_n=(1-\zeta^3)^2(1-\zeta^2)/((1-\zeta)^2(1-\zeta^6))$ with $\zeta=e^{2\pi i/q}$, drops the terms with even exponent by orthogonality against a character of conductor $q$, and applies Washington's formula $\big|\sum_{a\in(\Z/q)^\times}\chi(a)\log|1-\zeta^a|\big|=\sqrt q\,|L(1,\chi)|$ (\cite{Wash} Chapter~4, the $L(1,\chi)$ formula together with $|\tau(\chi)|=\sqrt q$), obtaining $|S(\chi)|=|1-\chi(3)|\sqrt q\,|L(1,\chi)|$ and $\prod_\chi|1-\chi(3)|=\Phi_{2^n}(1)=2$. Full proofs: Appendix~\ref{app:D}. $D_n>0$ follows also from $L(1,\chi)\ne0$ (Dirichlet), independently of Lemma~D.

\section{Certified computations}\label{sec:cert}
\subsection{Pipeline: untrusted generator, read-only verifier}
A generator (untrusted; Appendix~\ref{app:F}) searches each component lattice $\Lambda_f$ for a vector $a\in L_f\setminus\ell R_n$ with either $\hgt(\ell^{-1}H_na)<L_n$ (RHO route) or $T(a)=\sum_{j<2m}\exp(2y_j)<\barT$ with $y=\ell^{-1}H_na$ (T route; $\barT=33\cdot2^n$ for $n\ge3$ by \cite{KY} Thm.~2.3 and $17\cdot2^n$ for $n=2$ by \cite{MO3} Prop.~6.6; the verifier recomputes $\barT$ from $n$ and rejects a header that claims any other threshold), using LLL/BKZ and, when needed, exact SVP. Nothing produced by the generator is trusted. A separate verifier (the family verifier of Appendix~\ref{app:F}, $80$ lines, no search, no dependence on the generator) reads a witness file and recomputes from the definitions, in ball arithmetic at $256$ bits: the factorisation of $x^m+1$ over $\F_\ell$ (rejecting the file unless it is squarefree with all factors of degree $\ord_{2^n}(\ell)$ and unless the header fields $n,\ell,m,\deg,\#\text{components},\barT,L_n$ agree with the recomputation), the identity of each named factor by its full coefficient list, the exact membership $f\cdot a\equiv0\bmod(\ell,x^m+1)$, $a\notin\ell R_n$, the certified nonvanishing of some log coordinate, the route inequality with the ball's upper endpoint, and agreement of the claimed value with the recomputed one to $10^{-6}$. It returns \textsc{excluded} only if every component carries at least one accepted line and no line fails. \lbl{C}

\section{Limits of the method}\label{sec:limits}
\subsection{Scaling}\label{sec:scaling}
Theorem~\ref{thm:S0} and Cor.~\ref{cor:S1} are proved in Appendix~\ref{app:E}. The lattice half is the group lemma: $[R_n:L_f]$ is a power of the odd prime $\ell$, so $L_f\cap2^{t-1}R_n=2^{t-1}L_f$ (\lbl{F}: \path{smul_mem_iff_of_coprime}); under hypothesis (i) the filtered lattice is the exact dilate $2^{t-1}\Lambda_f$, and every relative minimum scales by $2^{t-1}$. The floor half has two lemmas: the depth-$t$ height floor $\hgt(u)\ge L_{n,t}$ for $u\in\RE\setminus\{\pm1\}$, $u\equiv1\pmod{2^t\mathcal O_n}$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:depthfloor}, Schinzel's inequality), and the odd-power depth transfer $x^\ell\equiv1\iff x\equiv1\pmod{2^t\mathcal O_n}$ for units $x$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:oddtransfer}; $2$ is totally ramified in $B_n$ with residue field $\F_2$, so $(\mathcal O_n/2^t\mathcal O_n)^\times$ is a $2$-group and an odd power map on it is a bijection, \lbl{F}: \path{WeberOddTransfer.pow_odd_eq_one_iff_of_two_pow_card}). The transfer is what allows the floor to be applied to the unit carrying the short vector ($r_a$, or $u_b$ in the base branch) when only its $\ell$-th power $u_a$ is known to be $\equiv1\pmod{2^t}$. The analytic half, $(2+\sqrt5)^{2^{t-1}}>2^t+\sqrt{4^t+1}$ for every $t\ge2$, is \lbl{F}: \path{ineq_t_general}; it says $L_{n,t}/2^{t-1}<L_n$, so the depth-$t$ contradiction needs a shorter vector than the plain one. Hypothesis (i) is certified for $t=2,3,4$ (Cor.~\ref{cor:S1}); for $t\ge5$ it is open, and we make no claim there.
\subsection{Class $1$} \lbl{E} For $\ell\equiv1\pmod{128}$ the components have degree $1$. For each scalar residue class of a component, the T route may use any lift $a+\ell z$, $z\in R_n$; the search performed used the centered representatives together with an explicit local $\pm\ell$-coordinate descent, and is not a full nonlinear closest-vector search. At $\ell=1000000513$ this search over all $64$ components found no witness under either floor (per-component minima $5.1\times10^4$--$2.3\times10^5$ against $4224$). This is a limitation of the implemented search, not of the T route: the class-$1$ case above $10^9$ and below the Cor.~\ref{cor:7} threshold is not settled by this method, and no statement about it is made.

\section{Characters of $G_n$ and Frobenius orbits}\label{app:C}
$(\Z/q)^\times=\{\pm1\}\times\langle3\rangle$ with $3$ of order $2^n$ (from $3^{2^k}\equiv1+2^{k+2}\pmod{2^{k+3}}$). Since $\sigma_{-a}=\sigma_a$ on $B_n$, $G_n=(\Z/q)^\times/\{\pm1\}$ is cyclic of order $2^n$, generated by the class of $3$, and $\tau=\sigma^{m}$ is the class of $1+q/2$. A character of $G_n$ is an even character of $(\Z/q)^\times$; it has conductor exactly $q$ iff it is nontrivial on $\{1,1+q/2\}$ iff $\chi(\tau)=-1$; then $\chi(3)^m=\chi(\tau)=-1$, so $\chi(3)$ is a primitive $2^n$-th root of unity, and the $m$ such characters give all $m$ primitive roots. Lemma~B is the orbit count of the same Frobenius on the roots of $\Phi_{2^n}$ over $\F_\ell$.
